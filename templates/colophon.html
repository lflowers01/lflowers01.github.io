I enjoy figuring out how things work, but I also care about how they look and feel. Building this site gave me a chance to work on both. I wanted somewhere to share my projects, photos, and music, and to keep adding things as I learn.

I tend to spend a lot of time on small details. Here, that has meant getting the text to sit between the lines on a note, making the sand pile up properly, and figuring out how much the ASCII should react when you move your mouse. I like being able to try an idea, see what feels wrong, and keep changing it until it works.

The design

The cutting mat, notes, and iPod came from wanting the page to feel a little like a desk. You can move things around, put on music, and draw in the empty space. The sand and Game of Life grew out of experimenting with what else those same pixels could do.

I kept the colors simple: black or dark green, muted green, warm white, and a little brown. The flower is from my previous website. Looking through the Purdue Hackers site and its webring gave me ideas for the ASCII and dither effects. The artwork and rendering here are separate implementations.

The pixel font is Departure Mono by Helena Zhang. The handwriting on the notes is Kalam by Indian Type Foundry, rendered as pixels. Both use the SIL Open Font License: Departure Mono license and Kalam license. The larger headings use Arial, and the reading text uses Verdana with system fallbacks.

The rings in the hero are a 3D shape drawn with characters on a fixed grid. You can rotate them and make ripples through the background. There is also a WALL-E hidden in the footer, using the ASCII reference I chose for the site. WALL-E belongs to Pixar/Disney.

What gets saved

Your theme, drawings, and the positions of the photos, notes, and iPod are saved in your browser. The drawings stay on your device; they are not uploaded or published. The clear button clears the drawing surface and leaves your arrangement alone. The published notes are the ones I add to the site.

The visit count is a shared total of accepted homepage visits, not a count of individual people. The counter stores the total and uses short-lived hashes to limit repeated requests, without saving raw IP addresses in its database. If it cannot load, it says “Unavailable.”

The player connects to SoundCloud when you use it. SoundCloud and the artists control which tracks are available, along with previews and ads. The waveform follows the track’s saved amplitude data at the current playback position. Shuffle works through the tracks before repeating; the playlist is a snapshot of the linked station, whose live recommendations can change.
