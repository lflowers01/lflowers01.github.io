Your own little corner of the internet.

A personal Web 1.0 home for Lucas Flowers: things you build, things in your head, a little music, and a robot happy to have visitors.

This draft picks a concrete direction so you have something to react to. It connects your earlier terminal, forest themes, desktop windows, games, and Webamp experiments with the new ideas from our research.

The next step is your feedback. We will revise this file until you like the plan. The full website comes after you say the plan is ready; this page does not start a build or publish anything.

Section 1A forest desktop, with a life of its own.

Recommended starting direction

Picture a handmade personal computer sitting somewhere between a forest cabin and an electronics bench. Spruce title bars, warm reading surfaces, low-color photographs, small pixel objects, and a moving ASCII robot. A little clutter around a clear, readable center.

The personality comes from your photos, projects, notes, favorite links, and odd discoveries. Robotics is one part of the home; there is room for music, trips, games, and whatever else you want to share.

Type and materials

Pixel lettering for short window titles and signs; comfortable sans-serif text for reading. Recover your older pixel fonts if their permissions fit. Dither photos with one shared palette. Use a small tile, beveled borders, underlined links, and original robot stamps.

Density and movement

More inhabited than the current landing page, with a few distinct panels visible together. One main animated artwork. Other motion answers a click or a tap. A Quiet mode stops decorative motion; sound always starts by choice.

If the forest desktop is the wrong feeling

Radio outpost: darker blue/purple, signal imagery, a listening station, a transmission portrait, and a station log. Pixel scrapbook: more illustration, stickers, photo scraps, shrines, and little collections. The content and core features below still work with either direction.

This plan's colors are a proposal. Its layout diagram shows hierarchy, not the finished artwork or final styling.

Grounding: your themes and earlier desktop, cinni's personal home, dithered visual systems.

Section 2What someone finds when they arrive.

The first screen introduces you and gives visitors obvious places to go. Window-like panels sit in a normal page layout. On desktop they make a small dashboard; on a phone they stack in a useful reading order.

First: your introduction and robot, followed by projects and notes.

Then: radio, neighbors, small collections, and the visible ring station.

Navigation: ordinary links and shareable locations. No mandatory boot screen or double-click.

Windows: fixed in the page by default. Dragging is an open choice, not a prerequisite for the aesthetic.

Section 3The things we will build.

These are the proposed features for the full first version. The build phases sequence them; they do not quietly drop the music or shared-data ideas.

Visual identity

Dithering

A dithered portrait and selected project thumbnails, prepared from your original images with one two- or four-color palette. Detail pages keep full-color images when engineering details matter. An optional before/after control can reveal the original portrait.

Learn from the image pipeline and your existing processed assets.

Signature interaction

A living ASCII robot

A small original robot whose eyes follow a pointer and whose expression changes on tap, keyboard activation, or a boop. A brief finite text reveal can introduce it. Visible Pause and Quiet controls keep the page comfortable; reduced motion starts with a still pose.

Start with a character grid and a few expressions. A river-sized ecosystem is an alternative if you prefer that to a mascot.

Learn from Anish's grid, Alicia's reveal, and ASCII rendering notes.

Portfolio

Project folders

A few real projects on the homepage, an archive of older work, and one honest space for future experiments. Each entry gets a title, date/status, short purpose, preview, and working detail link. Project pages show the build, photographs, lessons, and next step.

Choose which archived work belongs in the new introduction. Old “Present” labels will be checked with you before publication.

Ten real candidates already in your archive.

Living content

A public notebook

Short dated thoughts, observations, links, and rough ideas. Show the latest three on the homepage; give the full notebook permanent entries and a feed. A title is optional. You post by editing a small Markdown file locally or through GitHub's editor, previewing, and publishing.

No writing dashboard in the first version. If that workflow feels too slow, we can change it after trying a real post.

Brain-buffer recipe; feed from the same content.

Shared data

Boop the robot

One visible button changes the robot's expression and increments a real shared total. The label says “boops,” so it counts accepted actions honestly. Everyone reads the same database total; browser storage only remembers preferences.

Show waiting, confirmed, rate-limited, and offline states. The button stays useful as a local expression change when the service is unavailable, with no claim that a shared boop was saved. A classic pageview odometer is an alternative if you prefer it.

Shared-counter design.

Music

A listening window

Recommended: Webamp, opened on request, with a permitted skin and a short playlist you choose. Show track credits and a way to pause, seek, and change volume. It stays mounted while visitors move between the main homepage sections.

Phones get a player that fits, with native audio as a fallback. Full-page navigation to an archive or project article can stop playback; uninterrupted music across every route is a separate choice to settle before building. No music autoplays.

Webamp implementation notes; your earlier player experiment.

Discovery

One hidden drawer

A loose screw, tiny icon, or odd notebook link opens a secret page: an original robot blooper, a small repair toy, or a dated old game. Finding it awards a little stamp on this browser. A visible hint and keyboard/touch route make discovery possible without hover.

The exact hiding place and reward are open. The egg never hides the projects, contact links, or webring navigation.

Arhan's theatrical local effect; discovery stamps.

Web neighborhood

Buttons, links, and a ring station

An original 88×31 site button, a small credited shelf of friends and favorite places, a real last-updated note, RSS, and visible Previous / Purdue Hackers webring / Next links. A small changelog makes the page feel cared for.

Prepare the ring links during the build. Test them after the chosen URL is live, then follow the ring's joining process. Guestbooks and shoutboxes remain optional additions because they bring moderation work.

Button shelf; ring integration.

Optional accents to choose after the main direction feels right

A second night skin, a readable-font toggle, a tiny cursor-spark effect, an illustrated room entrance, a photo drawer, or a topic shrine. We can add these deliberately after the core composition is settled. Full OS simulation, multiple always-running effects, a custom audio engine, and public chat are outside this first proposal.

Section 5A small site underneath the personality.

Proposed implementation: a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript site with a small build step for Markdown content. Main sections share one page and hash links; longer notes, project stories, and the archive can have separate pages. Webamp is the main specialist dependency.

Proposed destinations

  • /Introduction, ASCII resident, project shelf, recent notes, radio, neighbors.
  • /#projects · /#notebook · /#radioMain-section navigation without replacing the player.
  • /projects/<project>/Real project stories and build logs.
  • /notes/ · /notes/<entry>/Notebook archive and permanent entries.
  • /archive/ · /feed.xmlOlder work and subscription feed.

Content and assets

Keep notes and project descriptions in Markdown with a small amount of metadata. Generate the index, latest entries, and feed from that same source. Keep original images separate from thumbnails and dithered exports.

Recover useful historical assets by commit without replacing this branch's research. Keep the research library and this plan available during development. No need for a database to store your writing.

Technical plan for the shared counter

Recommended backend: a Cloudflare Worker and one D1 counter row. A read endpoint returns the aggregate; a write endpoint accepts exactly one boop for the fixed counter. Use an atomic increment, server-side rate limits, bounded requests, and a private reset route. Keep credentials out of the browser.

The UI updates its number only after server confirmation. It handles uncertain writes without blind retries and shows an explicit offline state. Local cooldowns are convenience controls, not identity or abuse protection. Provisioning the actual service happens during the approved implementation, not through this plan.

Phone, keyboard, motion, and loading behavior

Stack the windows on small screens; keep titles and controls readable. Use real links and buttons, visible focus, a logical reading order, and a keyboard equivalent for meaningful pointer actions. The ASCII grid gets fewer cells instead of unreadably small text. Optional decoration is hidden from screen readers; the robot's meaningful state also appears as text.

Quiet mode pauses artwork and decorative movement. Reduced-motion visitors start still. Load the music library only on request, stop background animation when hidden, and use static posters for previews. Core introduction, projects, and notes remain readable without the interactive layer. Test the actual selected behaviors rather than promising that every reference site already does this.

Publishing and the existing site

Keep the current live site and main unchanged during design and development. Build and review on this branch. Before release, settle whether the new version replaces the main site, lives under a path, or has a different hostname. That choice affects asset paths and ring registration.

A branch alone is not a second hosted site. Release means building the output, checking actual links and metadata at the chosen URL, configuring the shared service, and then following the ring's enrollment steps. Nothing in this draft changes publishing settings.

Section 6How we get from this plan to the full thing.

  1. Revise and settle this plan.

    Choose the mood, density, signature artwork, and feature behavior. Keep editing this document until you say the direction is ready. This is where we are now.

  2. Build the visual shell and one real screen.

    Implement the palette, tile, frames, type, mobile layout, portrait treatment, and actual introduction. Review the composition before filling every panel.

  3. Bring in the content and publishing workflow.

    Add selected project stories, the older archive, notebook entries, navigation, contact/resume links, feed, neighbors, and metadata. Verify a new Markdown note can be published without editing layout code.

  4. Make the resident and discoveries work.

    Build the ASCII interaction, pause/quiet states, keyboard/touch controls, and hidden drawer. Keep the artwork bounded and the recovery route obvious.

  5. Connect music and the shared number.

    Integrate Webamp and permitted tracks, then the real boop service. Test pending/error/offline states, simultaneous increments, and rate limiting. A staged or fake value is never presented as a live shared total.

  6. Review the complete site, then release.

    Check phones, desktop, keyboard, reduced motion, asset permissions, missing media, performance, actual URLs, feed, and counter behavior. Publish to the agreed destination and test the webring links there before requesting membership.

Section 7What to react to first.

You can respond with section numbers, rough reactions, examples, or a list of things to keep/change. No need to answer every detail at once.

  1. The world: does the forest desktop feel like you?

    Keep it, move toward a radio outpost or scrapbook, or describe a different mix. Tell me whether you want denser and weirder, calmer, darker, brighter, or less robotics-focused.

  2. The central toy: a robot, an ASCII ecosystem, or something else?

    The robot and boop total connect naturally, but the art should be something you enjoy seeing and sharing.

  3. The layout: arranged windows or a more spatial room?

    Default is readable panels that stack on phones. Say if draggable windows, an illustrated scene, or a more cluttered personal-homepage composition are important to you.

  4. The everyday experience: what should feel different?

    For example: Winamp versus a custom cassette, boops versus visitor count, more writing versus more toys, or music continuing through every page. Also flag any feature you would remove.

Then: I update this plan, you review it again, and we repeat. When you say you like the plan and want the full site built, this becomes the build brief.
