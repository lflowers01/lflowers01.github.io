Draft 3

Website design plan

A Web 1.0 personal site with green as the primary color, brown surroundings and accents, pixel typography, dithered images, interactive ASCII, and space for your work and writing at Purdue.

This revision specifies the appearance and placement of the interface. The samples below use the proposed fonts and colors directly, so you can respond to what you see.

Design direction: green remains primary, with brown surroundings and trim; concrete type samples; more detailed desktop, mobile, and page layouts; neutral interface language; and a fresh project section focused on college-era work. High-school projects will not be imported into the new site's main content.

01 / DirectionGreen first, with brown and earth-tone accents.

Deep green title bars and selected controls, sage workspace surfaces, pale paper reading panels, and a brown textured surround. Brown also appears in borders and small details. Squared windows, visible underlined links, bitmap details, and low-color imagery establish the early-web character.

The layout should feel personal and deliberately arranged. It will introduce you as a college student, show current work when it exists, and give notes, music, and interactive elements their own places.

04 / Desktop compositionWhere everything goes on the homepage.

Proposed site width: 1120px maximum, centered with at least 24px outside space when available. A flexible main column sits beside a 272px utility column, separated by 16px. The name and current introduction remain the first things to read.

A
Top frame and navigation

Full width. Domain at left, optional page title at right. A 40–44px navigation row below: Home, Projects, Notes, About. Motion and audio state sit at the far right; they do not replace the main links.

B
Upper main column: introduction

Portrait at left, 96×128px in the full layout. Name, current Purdue introduction, and three direct contact/profile links at right. Allow about 200px for this panel, expanding naturally with text size. No oversized fullscreen hero.

C
Main column below: projects, then notes

Projects sit directly below the introduction. Use the empty state until current work is ready; later show at most two previews. The notes panel follows, with the latest three entries, dates, and an All notes link. Avoid tiny internal scroll areas.

D
Right column: ASCII, music, visits

The ASCII panel aligns with the top of the introduction. Reserve about 240px of height including controls. Music follows at roughly 180px; Visits follows at roughly 110px. These are starting sizes, not fixed clipping heights. The column scrolls with the page.

E
Bottom: links and ring navigation

A short row of credited site buttons and selected links, then the webring footer. Both span the page. Keep the ring controls visible and separated from experimental content; no hidden navigation or fake taskbar.

First-screen priority: introduction and contact links, ASCII display, project state, music entry. Notes can continue below the first screen. We will tune the exact vertical fit against a 1440×900 desktop viewport during the approved build.

05 / Mobile compositionOne column, with a deliberate reading order.

At approximately 760px and below, the final site becomes a single column with 12–16px outside space. Navigation wraps instead of disappearing into a menu. Fonts keep their readable sizes; the decorative frame becomes thinner.

What changes

  • The utility column is redistributed into the main reading order.
  • The name uses 33px Departure Mono. Body text remains 14–15px.
  • The ASCII grid gets fewer columns. It never forces horizontal page scrolling.
  • Project previews use the full content width; dates and status wrap naturally.
  • 88×31 images retain their original size, with larger linked hit areas.
  • Webamp gets a compact fallback if the full player cannot fit.

What stays consistent

The same green-led palette with brown accents, title bars, typefaces, link style, and content. No miniature desktop that requires pinching to operate.

Individual note

07 / BehaviorWhat the elements do, and how they look in each state.

Dithered images

Appearance: green/cream two- or four-color portrait and small previews; solid 1px frame. Behavior: a labeled Original image link can open the clear source image. Detailed schematics stay undithered.

Use one palette and crop convention across assets. The checker patterns in this plan demonstrate texture only, not a finished portrait.

Interactive ASCII

Appearance: dark green characters on a pale inset field, with two controls directly below. Proposed subject: a wireframe object or mechanism. The final subject is still open.

Behavior: pointer movement changes orientation; touch or arrow controls step the view. Pause stops motion; Reset restores the original view. Reduced motion starts still. A brief initial text reveal can be tried after the composition is approved.

Visit counter

Appearance: six odometer-style digit cells in the lower utility panel, labeled Visits. Use a text label and number that remain readable without decorative styling.

Behavior: a server stores accepted homepage-load increments and returns the total. It is not labeled unique people. Offline displays Unavailable or a clearly marked last-known value, never a fabricated zero. Preview/local sessions do not write to the production counter.

Music / Webamp

Appearance: a compact green-framed music panel with track state and an Open player control. The expanded player will use one selected green/brown-compatible skin; the exact permitted skin will be shown for review before integration.

Behavior: Webamp loads when requested and audio starts only after Play. The homepage player sits below the ASCII panel, never on top of the introduction by default. On small screens it becomes an in-flow panel or native audio fallback. Full-page navigation may stop playback unless we choose a persistent shell before implementation.

Notes

Appearance: newest-first entries separated by fine dotted rules, dates in Departure Mono, body in Verdana. Three recent entries on the homepage and a full chronological archive.

Behavior: one Markdown entry generates its permanent page, archive entry, homepage preview, and feed entry. Editing and publishing remain separate actions. Nothing in the layout depends on having many posts.

Easter egg

Appearance: one secondary graphic or link detail with an unobtrusive clue. Proposed result: a short alternate ASCII scene or hidden page using the same palette.

Behavior: optional, reversible, and available by keyboard/touch. Keep the exact reveal for a later design pass. No forced sound, page takeover, or requirement to solve it to access the site.

Webring and links

Appearance: a quiet full-width footer, with Previous / Purdue Hackers webring / Next as visible text links. A separate row holds selected 88×31 buttons with attribution.

Behavior: normal anchors that work without the interactive layer. Test recognition and follow enrollment instructions only after the eventual site is live.

08 / Content directionStart with where you are now.

The new site centers your college life, current interests, new projects, and ongoing writing. We will not populate the project section with high-school work to fill space.

At launch

  • A short current introduction and Purdue context.
  • Resume, GitHub, and email links you want public.
  • A new portrait or another image you choose.
  • A clear Projects empty state unless current work is ready.
  • A few real notes when you have something to post.
  • Music and selected links based on your current taste.

As the site grows

  • College projects, independent builds, or current experiments you choose to publish.
  • Dated process notes and photographs.
  • Personal writing beyond engineering.
  • Additional collections only when you want to maintain them.

The older archive remains in the repository and existing site history. It is not part of this new site's proposed navigation or default content.

Needed later: your current bio, preferred photo, up-to-date resume, music selection, and any current project you actually want featured. We can approve the visual plan before all that content is ready.

09 / Implementation sequenceDesign decisions first, then the full site.

  1. Refine this visual plan.

    Settle palette, font pairing, component treatment, desktop/mobile arrangement, and ASCII subject. No full-site implementation until you approve the plan.

  2. Implement and review the homepage composition.

    Use actual spacing, typography, responsive rules, image treatment, and college-era copy. Review the page at desktop and phone widths before building out the remaining destinations.

  3. Build the content templates.

    Projects, project details, notes, note details, About, and the feed. Use Markdown for authored entries. An empty section remains a designed state.

  4. Add interactive elements and services.

    ASCII input/pause/reset, the easter egg, Webamp, and a real visit counter. Use a small server endpoint with atomic increments, bounded requests, rate limits, and clear failure states.

  5. Verify and release to the agreed destination.

    Check actual fonts, links, keyboard/touch behavior, reduced motion, media, content updates, counter concurrency, and production asset paths. Settle the hosting URL and test ring controls before joining. The current site stays unchanged until the release is agreed.

Proposed implementation underneath the visuals

Static HTML/CSS/JavaScript with a small Markdown build step. Webamp is the specialist player dependency. Notes and projects stay in source files; browser preferences store theme/motion/volume only. A small Worker/D1 service is one option for the counter; server credentials never enter the browser.

Keep the research and plan during implementation. No archive import or full desktop window manager is assumed. Main-section links can use hashes; long entries get their own URLs. Choose whether music must persist across full-page navigation before finalizing routing.

10 / Next reviewWhat to judge in this revision.

The concrete decisions are now visible above. You can refer to any section or sample when giving feedback.

  1. Fonts: the actual Departure Mono and Verdana specimens, especially the name, window bars, and reading text.
  2. Colors and materials: green as the main color, brown in the surround and trim, pale reading surfaces, and texture density.
  3. Composition: introduction/projects/notes at left; ASCII/music/visits at right; links and webring across the bottom.
  4. Visual density: whether you want more imagery, a denser early-web layout, or a simpler arrangement.
  5. ASCII and player: the geometric display as the starting subject, and the compact panel leading to Webamp.

We will keep revising build.html. Once you approve the plan, it becomes the brief for the full implementation.
